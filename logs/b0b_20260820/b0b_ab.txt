CHECK 2 - D1 = S*(pw + k) + m, one (k, m) for every transaction
          (a shape check on the DIFFERENCE; D1 is not the pass)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  fitted from the first two distinct S:  k = 30   m = 5
  14/14 transactions match D1 = S*(pw + 30) + 5

  The WITHDRAWN II=1 endpoint is k = m = 0, i.e. D1 = I.  Over
  these 14 transactions that form gives 46,254 cycles; the
  measurement is 57,964 - +11,710 (+25.32%).
  csynth says the II really IS 1 (scan_init and scan_slide at
  II=1, iteration latencies 7 and 14, the same as the isq_init
  and isq_slide they replace), so the miss is entirely the
  per-scan constant that was never modelled.

  k = 30 AND m = 5 ARE NOT PROPERTIES OF THE PASS.  D1 carries
  the shadow's comparator, +2 per output row.  Take it out and
  the same fourteen numbers read S*(pw + 29) + th + 3 -- a
  th-dependent constant instead of a fixed one.  See check 3b.

CHECK 3 - D2 = -(rh*th*(tw + rw + W) + c*rh), one (W, c) for every
          transaction
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  fitted:  W = 24   c = 3
  14/14 transactions match

  THE PRE-REGISTRATION IS REFUTED, and this is the finding.
  logs/b0b_20260820/PREDICTION.txt registered W = 21, c = 0
  before the first build, straight from the model's four-way
  split of the fitted per-row term.  Measured: W = 24, c = 3.
  Over these 14 transactions the prediction is short by 5,190 cycles.
  Only the SUM of that four-way split ever had evidence.  This
  is the first of its four terms to be measured, and the other
  three now sum to 2*tw + 2*rw + 9 rather than + 12.  WHICH of
  them was over-attributed is NOT established.

  W = 24 SURVIVES THE COMPARATOR AND c = 3 DOES NOT.  The
  nuisance is proportional to rh; W's regressor is rh*th and th
  varies across the suite, so nothing rh-proportional can move
  W.  The refutation above therefore holds either way.  c is
  the removal's own share plus the comparator's 2.
  Note tw + rw = pw + 1, so the measured cost is rh*th*(pw + 25) + 3*rh:
  it depends on the PATCH WIDTH alone, which is what the two
  deleted loops actually scan (tw priming + rw - 1 sliding).

CHECK 3b - the D1/D2 split is NOT identified, and here is the proof
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  csynth locates the shadow's comparator in norm_cols at +2
  cycles per call, and norm_cols runs once per output row.  So
  shift 2*rh from D1 to D2 and refit:

    as measured       D1 = S*(pw + 30) + 5
                      D2 = -[rh*th*(tw + rw + 24) + 3*rh]
    comparator out    D1 = S*(pw + 29) + th + 3
                      D2 = -[rh*th*(tw + rw + 24) + 1*rh]

  14/14 transactions match the decontaminated D1 form
  14/14 transactions match the decontaminated D2 form
  14/14 transactions still sum to the SAME D

  BOTH PAIRS FIT EXACTLY, because they are the same fourteen
  numbers rearranged.  The co-simulation constrains D and not
  the split, so NEITHER pair is a measurement of the pass or of
  the removal.  The decontaminated one is the leading candidate
  -- it is where csynth puts the comparator -- and it is NOT
  frozen: it rests on a scheduler estimate rather than on RTL,
  and it cannot exclude a further per-invocation constant, which
  trades against the +3 with no way to tell them apart.

  A COMPARATOR-FREE CONTROL WOULD SETTLE IT AND CANNOT EXIST.
  The shadow's job is to keep both copies of the statistics
  live; an unread copy is dead code and gets deleted, leaving
  b2ctl or b0b.  Some consumer must read both, and the two array
  reads already there are the cheapest one.
